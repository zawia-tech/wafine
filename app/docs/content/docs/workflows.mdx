---
title: Workflows
description: Paced auto-replies that answer your contacts with a human-like delay.
---

Workflows are simple automatic replies that run on your session. They answer known
contacts with fixed text, with no AI involved, and send at a human pace.

## Built-in rules

| Rule | When it replies | Reply | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ping | The message is exactly `ping` | `pong` | none |
| Greeting | The message is exactly `hello`, `hi`, `salam` or `bonjour` | A short greeting that says a person will get back to you | Once per contact per 24 hours |

Matching ignores case and a trailing `!`, `.`, `,` or `?`. Anything else is left for you to answer.

## Who gets a reply

Workflows reply only to contacts on your allowlist. They never reply to:

- your own messages;
- groups and status updates;
- messages that are not text, such as images or voice notes;
- contacts that are not on your allowlist.

## Turn a rule on or off

All rules start **off**.

1. Open **Workflows** in the web app.
2. Switch on the rule you want.
3. Send `ping` from an allowlisted contact to check that it answers.

Switch the rule off the same way to stop it.

## How pacing works

Auto-replies are designed to look like a person typing, not a bot:

1. WaFine waits a short "reading" delay, then a "typing" delay that grows with the reply length.
2. Replies on one session go out one at a time, a few seconds apart.
3. Each contact gets at most two auto-replies per minute, and each session at most six.
4. When a limit is reached, the extra reply is **dropped**, not queued for later.
5. If WhatsApp or WaFine signals that the session is sending too fast, auto-replies pause
   for a while. A failed reply is never retried.

Auto-replies also count against your session's send budget.

## Good practice

- Keep the greeting rule for contacts who write to you first; it never starts a conversation.
- Answer real questions yourself soon after the greeting, as it promises.
- Do not enable OpenWA's own auto-reply rules on the same number if you self-host:
  two engines answering the same message look like spam.
